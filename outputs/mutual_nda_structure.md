# Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement — Standard Structure

A mutual NDA is the disclosure-gate contract two parties sign before sharing sensitive information — the standard first step once a capacity-exchange deal moves past the anonymous stage and both sides need to discuss real specifications, drawings, or pricing. Structurally, mutual NDAs used across manufacturing sourcing, product development, and business-partnership contexts share the same core sections:

## 1. Parties & Effective Date
Full legal names of both organizations, the effective date, and (often) a defined "Purpose" — the specific reason confidential information is being exchanged (e.g., "evaluating a potential subcontract machining arrangement").

## 2. Definition of Confidential Information
What counts as protected: typically technical data, drawings, specifications, pricing, customer/supplier lists, and business plans, disclosed in written, oral, or visual form. Well-drafted NDAs require oral disclosures to be confirmed in writing within a set window (commonly 10–30 days) to count as protected.

## 3. Exclusions
Standard carve-outs: information already public, already known to the receiving party before disclosure, independently developed without reference to the disclosed information, or received legitimately from a third party.

## 4. Obligations of the Receiving Party
- Use the information only for the stated Purpose
- Not disclose it to third parties without consent
- Limit internal access to employees/contractors with a genuine need to know, who are themselves bound by confidentiality
- Apply at least the same care used to protect the receiving party's own confidential information (a common minimum standard, not a specific technical control)

## 5. Term and Survival
Two separate clocks: how long the agreement is active (commonly 1–3 years), and how long the confidentiality *obligation* survives after that (commonly 2–5 years, sometimes indefinite for trade secrets specifically).

## 6. Return or Destruction of Materials
On request or termination, the receiving party returns or destroys disclosed materials (and copies), sometimes with a certification requirement.

## 7. No License / No Obligation to Transact
Explicit statement that sharing information creates no license to use it commercially and no obligation for either party to actually enter a deal — important in a marketplace context where an NDA is a precondition to *discussing* a deal, not a commitment to *make* one.

## 8. Remedies
Acknowledgment that a breach may cause irreparable harm not fully compensable by damages alone, entitling the disclosing party to seek injunctive relief in addition to damages.

## 9. Governing Law & Jurisdiction
Which province/state's law governs and where disputes are resolved — for a Canadian manufacturing marketplace, typically the province where the disclosing party (or the marketplace operator) is based.
