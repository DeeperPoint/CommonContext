## **Heavy Haul Transport & Logistics Guide (2025)** 

_A practical reference for moving over-dimensional and heavy industrial machinery across borders. Intended for Service Providers (carriers, freight forwarders, and customs brokers) operating on machinery trading platforms._ 

## **1. The Service Provider Role** 

Service Providers facilitate the physical fulfilment of a machinery sale. They do not buy or sell the Goods; they are engaged by the Seller or Buyer to move, inspect, or clear equipment. Distinct service roles include: **transport carrier** , **rigging / heavy-lift specialist** , **machinery inspector** , **customs broker** , and **cargo insurer** .

## **3. Service Regions** 

Carriers declare the regions they serve: North America, South America, Europe, Middle East, 

Africa, South Asia, East Asia, Southeast Asia, and Oceania. Cross-border capability (Yes / No) and common lanes (e.g. Hamburg–Gdansk, Houston–Veracruz) should be stated in the provider profile. 

## **4. Documentation & Customs** 

Cross-border machinery movement requires a commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and where applicable a certificate of origin and import permit. Customs brokers handle duties, taxes, and tariff classification (HS codes) at origin and destination. 

## **5. Carrier Compliance & Insurance** 

Carriers should hold relevant credentials: DOT Compliant (US), ADR (EU dangerous goods), TIR Carnet (transit), ISO 9001, and C-TPAT (supply-chain security). Cargo insurance coverage, expressed in USD, must be adequate to the declared value of the machinery in transit. 

## **6. Headquarters Countries** 

Carriers on the platform are commonly headquartered in the United States, Canada, Mexico, Germany, Netherlands, Poland, UAE, Singapore, Australia, or Brazil.
